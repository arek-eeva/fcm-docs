Every reference and instruction page for your Chief of Staff, on one page. You don't need to read it all. Take the first row in your first week and come back to the rest when a question actually comes up.

Your first week
These four, in this order. Nothing else matters yet.
  • Plain-English Glossary →
    Every word you'll meet, explained without jargon: session, prompt, ingest, memory, skill, playbook, agent, connector, Terminal, Git. Start with the first six.
  • Your Homework →
    What to gather before we build, so your assistant knows your business from day one instead of spending a fortnight catching up.
  • Install the Reader →
    Getting set up on your own machine, step by step, with nothing assumed about what you already know.
  • Twelve Jobs, One Memory →
    The idea behind the whole thing, written for someone who has already had a go with AI and wants to know why this is different.
Running it day to day
The six reference cards. Keep them open for the first month.
  • Driving Claude Code →
    The controls below the chat box: plan and auto mode, shaping a plan before it runs, choosing the model, the context meter, previewing changes.
  • Organising Your Workspace →
    Keeping the session list tidy and letting it work while you're away: status dots, forking, pinning, renaming, groups, scheduled routines.
  • Briefing Your Chief of Staff →
    How to tell it what it needs to know, and why a good brief is the difference between useful work and generic work.
  • Building Something →
    Going from an idea to a working tool you actually use, which is the moment the whole thing clicks.
  • Getting Unstuck →
    What to do when it does something odd, forgets something, or stops. Almost always fixable in a minute once you know where to look.
  • Backing It Up →
    Keeping your work safe, so nothing is ever really lost and you can go back if something breaks.
Going further
Once it's running and you want more out of it.
  • Connecting Your Tools →
    Plugging your email, calendar and other software in, so your assistant works inside them instead of you copying between tabs.
  • Skills for Inspiration →
    What other people have built, as a prompt for what yours could do. Useful when you know it's capable of more but can't picture what.
