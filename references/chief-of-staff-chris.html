The questions you didn't ask

You spent the call on the architecture and never once asked me about data handling. You do insider risk for a living, so I assume that's coming rather than absent. Here it is before you have to ask.

What is actually true, including the parts that don't help me
What it can reach
Whatever the account you connect can reach, and nothing else. That's the real thing to design around, far more than where anything is hosted. We scope it to named sources, one credential at a time, issued by you.
Where it runs
On your machine, in one folder. Plain files and rules you can read. The model is rented; the system is owned.
Data residency
The one that doesn't suit me. Processing does not happen in Australia today. There is no Australian data zone for these models. If any of your clients carry contractual Australian residency requirements, that is a hard constraint and you should know it now rather than mid security review.
Training and retention
On commercial terms your content isn't used to train models and is held for a short set window. That turns on which plan you're actually running, because consumer plans are governed differently. We check it together at go-live rather than assume it.
What's left on the machine
It keeps plain-text logs of its own sessions locally, in a known folder, for a number of days you set. That sits outside any tenant control your IT has built. I'd rather say it here than have you find it in month two.
Prompt injection
Incoming email and documents are attacker-controlled input. There are rules to detect and refuse instructions embedded in content, and approval gates mean a bad instruction still can't send or spend on its own. It is reduced, not solved. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling you something.
Formal security evidence
I can't give you certifications, a completed vendor questionnaire or contractual residency. Fifth Castle is a two-person business, not a certified vendor. What I can do is build to the controls you specify and bring your own people in early.
The uncomfortable one
An agent makes existing oversharing more efficient. It doesn't create access that wasn't already there, but it does raise the impact of access control that was already too loose. That's a finding about the environment, not about the tool.

None of that is a sales problem for me. You're the one person in my pipeline who will actually check, and a proposal that skipped this would be worth less to you than one that doesn't.

One seat now, three later

Each of you gets your own Chief of Staff. Not one system with three logins. Yours knows your clients, your pipeline and how you sell; Dave's knows delivery and operations; your third partner's knows what he's building. That's the whole reason you said doing all three at once would be worse.

The connecting layer between them is separate work, and I'm not quoting it here. It's the shared brief, the decisions log, and the gates that stop three systems overwriting each other's work. It's real engineering rather than a setting, and its size depends on how much the three of you genuinely need to share. We scope it when there are two of you running, not before.

How we do it

Four build sessions and two follow-ups on Google Meet, roughly five hours in the room across a fortnight. You do most of the work with me guiding, because a system you watched someone else build is one you can't extend.

    Session one

    Install, and teach it your business

    The folder goes on your machine and you unpack it, so you've seen every part before it does anything. Then it learns ShadowSight: how you talk, how you sell, what a good client looks like. Nothing is connected in this session, on purpose.
  • 2
    Session two

    Connect what you choose, then try to break it

    One credential at a time, each one issued by you and understood before it's live.     Driving it properly

    The difference between using it and running it. When to let it go and when to hold it, how to manage several jobs at once, and how to correct it so the correction sticks. This is the session that decides whether you still need me in three months.

And the honest bit about week one

The front of this is the heaviest. Keys, logins, teaching it things you've never had to write down. It feels like work before it feels like relief, and it's usually somewhere in the second week that it clicks. When it does, expect to lose a few evenings. Everyone I've built with hits the same stretch where they can suddenly do the things they'd parked for years, and they do all of them at once. Worth knowing before it happens rather than during.

What happens when the models change?

They're rented and the system is owned. Everything lives in plain files on your machine, so any capable model can be pointed at it. New releases improve the asset instead of obsoleting it. You'll want to re-point it once or twice a year, and that's an afternoon, not a rebuild.

Could our own developer just build this?

Your third partner probably could, eventually. What you'd be buying is the fortnight instead of the six months, and the fact that his time is worth more on your own applications. If he'd rather build it himself, that's a genuinely fine outcome and I'd say so.

Is this just ChatGPT with extra steps?

Chat is good and its memory is better than people give it credit for. The difference is that it remembers things about you, and this holds your business. A chat window exists while you're typing into it; this reads its own instructions, keeps its own records, and works on things while you're somewhere else.

What do I need on my side?

A paid Claude or ChatGPT plan in ShadowSight's name, at the hundred-dollar-a-month tier rather than the twenty. That's what gives it room to build rather than chat, it's paid to the vendor rather than to me, and it isn't included below. I'd rather you saw that recurring cost here than met it in session one.
