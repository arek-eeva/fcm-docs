Your homework

This is what to gather before session one. Rough is fine. Don't tidy anything up, don't turn it into a document, and don't spend an evening on it. Half an hour of copying things into a folder is the whole job, and we work with whatever you bring.

Everything here points at the two things you named on the call: getting the marketing running without you starting it, and taking the corralling off your plate.

Set up first · five minutes

1. Get a Claude subscription on the Max tier

You asked whether Pro was enough. Pro runs it, but the usage ceiling is low enough that you can hit it partway through a build session, which wastes the session. Max, on the 5x tier, about USD $100 a month. That's the figure in the proposal. You can drop back to Pro later if your real use turns out lighter.

2. Make one folder on your laptop

Call it whatever you like. Everything below goes in it, and that folder becomes the thing we install into. Nothing goes to a cloud service, and nothing needs ERA's IT for session one.

What to put in it

Writing that sounds like you

Three or four pieces you'd be happy to be judged on. A proposal you've sent, an email to a CFO, a summary you wrote for a client, a LinkedIn post if you have any. This is what teaches it your voice, and it's the difference between outreach that sounds like you and outreach that sounds like a marketer. You told me you didn't want the second one.

What you have actually delivered

The raw material for the marketing build. Categories you've worked in, the shape of a typical project, and the results where you're comfortable stating them. Anonymised is fine and probably necessary. This matters more than the writing samples: outreach written from a real track record is specific, and specific is the only thing that works when you dislike sounding transactional.

One client project, start to finish

The specification for the corralling build, so pick the one you'd most want run properly next time. What you sent the analysts at the start, your stakeholder mapping, one fortnightly review, and one status update. If the project office lives in a set of files you rebuild each time, that set of files is exactly what I want to see.

One Friday call, recorded

A single Teams transcript from one of your analyst calls. One is enough. It's how we prove the thing can read what you already produce and turn it into the update you currently write by hand.

How you're set up commercially

How an ERA project is structured and charged, in whatever form you have it. It needs this to talk sensibly about your own offer.

One thing to decide

What you're going to call it

You'll be talking to it every day, so it needs a name. It can be a human name or a role. One client called his the Director because he didn't want a person's name on it. Mine is Kody. There's no wrong answer, and it's a surprisingly useful decision to have made before we start.

What to leave out

You sit inside other companies' profit and loss accounts under your licence with ERA, so this matters more for you than for most.

Don't bring client financial data. Neither of the two builds needs it, and we agreed on the call to start where the sensitivity is low and widen it when you're ready. If a document you're bringing has a client's name, their numbers, or anything identifying in it, swap in a placeholder or cut that section. Don't spend time on it, just check before it goes in the folder.

The same goes for anything covered by an ERA confidentiality obligation. If you're unsure about a particular document, leave it out and ask me in session one.
