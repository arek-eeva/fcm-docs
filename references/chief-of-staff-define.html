Two Chiefs of Staff, and the layer between them.

Tony, you came at this from the infrastructure side, which is the right end to hold it by. This page covers both halves: what the thing actually does, for Joe, and what it does to a tenant, for you and Kevin. Written to be forwarded, and short enough that it will be.

What it is

A Chief of Staff is Claude Code, Anthropic's desktop application, working inside one folder on your own machine. There's no new platform to log into and no service of ours holding your data. It reads and writes real files in that folder, under your direction.

What makes it a Chief of Staff rather than a chat window is the folder's contents: a written operating brief for how your business runs, a memory of what's been decided and why, and a set of procedures it follows. It arrives knowing your business because you and I write that down together. Then it drafts, reconciles, chases, prepares and remembers, and it stops and asks before anything leaves the building.

The measure of a good one is that it holds the threads you're currently holding in your head.

The shape for two co-founders

This is the part that has not been built before, and I would rather say so than pretend otherwise.

A Chief of Staff is built for one person's own use: your voice, your judgement, your access. Two founders is therefore two builds, not one shared account, because a system that averages two people's judgement is useful to neither.

What sits between them is a shared layer: the sources of truth you both read, a decision log so neither of you is guessing at what the other agreed, and the queues and gates that stop two systems writing over each other. I've designed that layer on paper and never run it live with two founders in one business.

Not the same shape as what you deploy

Worth naming early, because conflating the two would waste the meeting. What you build for clients is the first-party Microsoft 365 connector: Claude reaching into a tenant through Graph, governed by tenant consent and app assignment. A Chief of Staff is a different architecture. It's Claude Code running a founder's own operating system in a folder on their own machine, and it connects outward to whatever it's permitted, rather than living in the tenant.

Different shapes, overlapping controls, and neither replaces the other. If anything the interesting question is what happens when both are in the same business.

For the infrastructure side

Kevin, this section is for you, and it's deliberately the honest version rather than the reassuring one.

  • I never claim compliance. We build to the controls you approve. Compliance depends on your licensing, configuration, contracts and data class, none of which I control.
  • The access problem is real and I'll concede it first. The agent sees what the signed-in person can see. For most founders that's effectively everything, so scoping reduces the blast radius rather than removing it.
  • Read-first by default. Search and read run freely, and anything that sends, spends, publishes or deletes stops and asks. Write capability gets turned on deliberately, one capability at a time, never by omission.
  • Scoped file and mail access. A single dedicated site rather than tenant-wide read, and mailbox access held to named mailboxes. Standard Microsoft mechanisms, documented by Microsoft, and yours to grant or refuse.
  • Prove the boundary rather than trust it. Before go-live we test that it can't reach what it shouldn't, and write down the result.
  • It writes plaintext transcripts to the machine. Session records land in a local folder, thirty days by default and adjustable. They sit outside every tenant control you've built, so I'd rather you heard it from me than found it.
  • A named rollback owner, agreed before go-live rather than during. You'll have your own sequence for that and I'd rather use yours than invent one.

I can't sign a vendor security questionnaire. Fifth Castle Media is a two-person video and systems business, not a certified vendor, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

Three things nobody has solved

Including me. You already know two of these, because you raised them.

  • Derived records

    Every summary, brief and note the system writes is a new record holding extracted sensitive content, and it inherits the permissions of wherever it was written rather than of the source. Nothing alerts on it. The best mitigation I have is deciding up front where outputs land and applying the same controls there.

  • Prompt injection through inbound mail

    An agent reading a mailbox is reading attacker-controllable text. Approval gates before anything leaves or changes are the mitigation, and they aren't a cure. Anyone telling you this is solved is selling.

  • What lands on the endpoint

    Related to the transcripts above, and broader. The device needs to be managed, encrypted and protected to your standard, not to one I invent. I have no visibility into whether it is, so I ask rather than assume.
