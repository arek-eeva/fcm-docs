Skills for inspiration

Once your Chief of Staff knows your business, you teach it skills. A skill is a routine it runs by name, so you stop explaining the same task from scratch every time.

Here's a sense of what I've built and use across my own businesses, grouped by what they do. None of it is off-the-shelf. Each one is shaped to the work it serves, which is the point. Treat this as a menu of ideas for yours, not a shopping list.

Ask your Chief of Staff to build you any of them. Describe the job the way you'd explain it to a new starter, out loud rather than typed, and it writes the skill.

Writing and content

  • Draft or rewrite a page, an email, or a post in your voice.
  • Run several competing versions of an important piece, score them, and merge the best (good for a launch email or a landing page).
  • Turn a recording, a voice note, or rough bullets into posts shaped for each platform.
  • Plan a run of posts that gives far more than it asks, so the occasional promotion actually lands.
  • Tighten and edit a draft you already have.
  • Build a full email sequence: a welcome, a nurture, a win-back.

Sales and the pipeline

  • Research a prospect before a call so you walk in informed.
  • Prepare the questions for a call (the good ones let them do the talking).
  • Turn a call into clean notes and a list of next actions.
  • Review your pipeline and tell you who needs a second touch.
  • Chase what's outstanding, work or money, in your tone.

Proposals and quotes

  • Turn a brief or a call into a priced proposal or quote, ready for you to check.
  • Build a branded pitch document or a one-pager.

Research and thinking

  • Produce a deep, fact-checked research report with its sources.
  • Interrogate a half-formed idea with hard questions until it's clear (this kit's setup grill is a cousin of that one).
  • Build a small panel of customer personas and have them react to your marketing before it ships.
  • Read a book or a long document into clean notes: the key ideas, the quotes worth keeping, and what to act on.
  • Turn a video or a talk into notes and its key moments, so you get the substance without watching the lot.

Money and decisions

  • Walk a real decision to a clear call, then set a date to look at it again so it doesn't quietly slip (your kit ships with this one).
  • A monthly snapshot of cash in and out, and a simple forecast of how many months you're covered.
  • Model a personal money decision, a big purchase, house math, a cash-flow stretch, on the numbers before you commit.

Documents and admin

  • Create and edit Word, PowerPoint, Excel and PDF files properly.
  • Prepare a payroll run or process a timesheet.
  • Write your Monday or Friday brief, or a month-end summary.
  • Read a contract and flag what to look at, then send anything real to your lawyer.

Brand and quality

  • Check any draft against your voice and standards before it goes out.
  • Hold your brand guidelines in one place and apply them to everything.

Media and design

  • Generate images or short video.
  • Make branded animated titles and lower-thirds for your videos.
  • Produce clean product or lifestyle shots.

Operations

  • Onboard a new client: set up their folder and update your trackers in one step.
  • Log a payment across all your records at once.
  • Refresh a simple dashboard of where things stand.

How skills grow

You don't build these all at once, and you shouldn't. The rule is simple: once you've done a task by hand three times, that's the signal to make it a skill.

Your kit already includes nineteen of them, plus three reviewers that check work before you see it. reference/Skills-Catalogue.md inside your own folder lists every one, what it's for, and then the good skills other people have published free, with how to add them safely. Read that when you want the real inventory. This page is the wider menu of what's possible.

Add the next one when a real job calls for it, not before. They also get sharper over time as you use them and tell your assistant what to fix.

When one of these sparks an idea for your work, say so in a session. Describe what you do now, by hand, and let your assistant turn it into a skill while you talk.
