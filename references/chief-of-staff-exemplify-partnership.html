Chief of Staff · For Ed Frame, Exemplify

You said you reckon there are opportunities. Here are the three I can see.

Written while we were on the call, which is the only honest way to show you how fast this part gets done. It's the shape of a partnership, not an offer. The commercial terms aren't set yet and I'll say exactly where that line sits rather than improvise numbers at you.

Why Exemplify specifically

You sell systems into the same businesses I do, and you sell them to the person who signs. A Platinum HubSpot partner running RevOps across Australia and New Zealand already holds the two things I don't have at scale: a client book of owner-operators, and their trust on the question of "should we put this in".

What you don't have is someone who installs an operating layer on top of the stack you've just built them. That's the gap as I see it from the outside, and it looks narrow enough that our scopes don't collide. You'd know better than I would whether that's true, and the version of it worth asking out loud is whether anyone at Exemplify would rather build this than install mine. I'd genuinely rather hear yes today than find out in three months.

The three shapes

Roughly in order of how much paperwork each needs before it could run. I have no fixed view on which is right and I'd rather fit your model than propose one.

  • One

    A channel that runs both ways

    If I meet a business that needs real RevOps or a HubSpot implementation done properly, it goes to you. If you meet an operator who'd benefit from a Chief of Staff, you bring me in. The split stays clean because the scopes don't overlap: I license the operating layer and train the person, you scope, build and support everything on your side and price it yourself. What a referral is worth in each direction is one of the open items below, and I'd rather set it with you than announce it at you.
  • Two

    Joint delivery on the same client

    You implement the stack, I install the operator on top of it, and the client gets one sequence rather than two vendors arriving in a random order. This is the version I'm most curious about, because the failure mode you and I both watch for is the same one: a client with a beautifully configured system nobody actually operates.
  • Three

    Exemplify installs it

    You and your team run builds for your own client base under an installer arrangement. Highest ceiling, slowest to stand up, and the one with real paperwork behind it. The standard licence covers a business running its own build; an installer agreement is a different document and it doesn't exist yet. That's mine to sort out, and I'd rather sort it than hand-wave it.

The models are rented. The system is owned. That distinction is what makes the licensed part licensable, and it's the whole reason two businesses can sell into one client without arguing about what they're selling.

What goes the other way

A channel I only take from isn't a partnership, so here's a real problem of mine in your exact domain, on the table first.

My own booking doors are a mess. There's a HubSpot meetings link still live in my email signature that I stopped watching months ago, and it's still producing leads. One of my current Chief of Staff prospects came in through it, on a referral, through a door I wasn't instrumented on. I have no attribution on it, no idea what else has come through, and my own marketing runs on a platform that competes with yours, so I'm not going to pretend I've got this solved.

That's the sort of thing I'd want to trade on. You look at my funnel, I put my system in front of yours, an hour of each other's time, and neither of us invoices for that first pass. If either side turns into real work after that, it gets quoted like real work.

What's decided, and what isn't

The important half of this page. I'd rather draw this line now than have you assume a number I haven't set.

  • Decided. The seat price is $5,500 ex GST, $6,050 inc GST per person. Four structured sessions plus two follow-ups, taking roughly four and a half to six hours of the operator's time across the build.
  • Decided. Payment is 50/50. Half to lock dates, half on go-live.
  • Decided. The guarantee. Two weeks or more after the first session, if you tell me what isn't working and why it can't be resolved, and it then can't reasonably be resolved, the second payment is waived. You keep the kit either way.
  • Not decided. The revenue share on a partner-introduced client. There's no agreed referral fee and no partner margin, because I haven't set one yet.
  • Not decided. Who owns the client in each of the three shapes above.
  • Not decided. What an installer's own build costs them, which is the question that makes shape three real or not.

Those three are scheduled work for September, and they were on that list before today. I'm not going to invent them on a call with a friend, because a number I make up now is one we'd both have to live with later.

What I'd suggest next

Take the seat yourself first. Not as a qualifying hoop, but because a partnership built off a demo has us both guessing: you at what you'd be installing, me at what to charge you for it. Live in one for a month and you'd know which of the three shapes is worth the paperwork, and you'd be selling something you run your own business on.

Then a separate conversation on the commercials, with your corrections in it. You price implementation work for a living and I don't, so your read on what a partner needs to make this worth carrying is worth more to me than my own guess at it.

Two dates, and I'd like both in the calendar before we hang up. One for your build starting, which I'll pick off my actual delivery queue rather than off enthusiasm, and one for the partnership conversation once you've lived in it. The second one is useless without the first.

That's the shape of it. Push on the parts that are wrong, particularly the split table, because that's the piece I've only tested against one other partner.
