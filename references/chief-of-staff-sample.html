What I heard from you

Correct me on any of this before we start. Getting it wrong here is cheap; getting it wrong in session four isn't.

  • You are the only place the whole picture exists

    Somebody asks where a piece of work stands and the honest answer is that nobody can tell them but you. That isn't a reflection on your team. What they'd need is spread across a mailbox, your head, a folder somebody named badly two years ago, and a conversation you had in the car. Eight people, one index, and the index is you.

  • Every chat starts from nothing, and you're the one filling the gap

    You open a new conversation and spend the first few minutes saying who you are, what the business does and what you're trying to get done, before you can ask the question you came with. Do that four times a day for a year and you've built nothing. The tool is capable and it's amnesiac, and the memory is you.

  • What it writes is competent, and it isn't yours

    You've asked one tool or another to draft something and got back a paragraph that could have come from anybody. So you rewrite it in your own words, and the rewrite takes longer than writing it cold would have. That's the ceiling on any tool with no idea who you are, and no amount of prompting gets you past it.

  • There's a job you do the same way every time, and it lives in a spreadsheet

    Scoping and quoting a new piece of work, in your case. A dozen or so steps, two other people, and a shared sheet that stops matching reality the first week anybody gets busy. You've tried two project tools on it and neither fitted, because the shape of the job is yours rather than theirs.

  • Marketing happens, and nobody can tell you whether it worked

    It goes out on a schedule because stopping feels worse than continuing, and nobody has ever had a spare week to work out which half of it earns its place. It's a real problem and it isn't the first thing we build. I'll say why further down.

How this document got made

This is the part worth watching on the 26th, because it's the mechanism rather than the pitch.

A real proposal like this one starts as a recording of a conversation. Nobody transcribes it and nobody fills in a template. The system reads the whole call, checks what it hears against what it already holds about the business, the price list and every prior conversation, then writes the document, argues with a couple of its own claims, and runs the result past two separate checks before I ever see it. The number is verified against the price list rather than remembered. If it can't find something, it says so instead of inventing it.

The reason it can do that is the six weeks that came before. It knows how I write, what I charge, what I've promised other people, and which claims I'm not allowed to make. A blank chat has none of that and never will, however good the model gets. That's the difference this whole thing turns on, and it's why the first session is the heavy one.

This particular document is the exception: no real conversation sits behind it, because Joe isn't real. It was assembled from the patterns that came up in five separate calls, which is why it reads a little more general than yours would.
