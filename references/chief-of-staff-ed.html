The handover is the part that goes first

You described the loop precisely: you have a conversation with a client, then you're writing a Slack message, then you're doing a handover, when what should happen is that the transcript goes to Rosie and she can go back and ask it anything she needs.

That's not a wish, it's a fortnight of setup. The recording already exists, whether it comes from Gemini or the HubSpot note taker. What's missing is something that reads it the moment it lands, knows which client it belongs to, files it where Rosie can reach it, pulls out what you committed to, and puts those commitments somewhere that chases them. You stop being the transport layer between your own meeting and your own team.

I'm not describing a feature I'd build for you. It's how I ran the call we just had. This document exists because the system read our transcript, not because I sat down to write it.

"It's not in my tone of voice"

You said that about the note taker's summarise-this-email output, and then the sharper version: it's not an email that I would send anyway. That's the real objection to every AI writing tool you've been shown, and it's why the generic ones get abandoned about three weeks in.

The difference here is what happens to a correction. When you change a line in something it drafted, that change doesn't evaporate when the chat window closes. It gets written down as a rule, and the next draft already has it. Correct a person and it fades. Correct a system and it compounds, which is why the thing gets more like you the longer you use it rather than less.

The honest version: week one it will not sound like you. It sounds like you somewhere around week three, and only because you spent week one telling it when it didn't.

You and Rosie, without a team account

You asked how you both get access to the same things when you're both on Claude but you don't have a team account. There are three answers and they're deliberately in order of cost.

1
Share a folder. The system lives in a folder on your machine that syncs to your Google Drive. You share one sub-folder, transcripts say, and Rosie has everything it files there. She can't reach the memory, the rules or anything else. This costs nothing and it's usually most of what people actually wanted.
2
Build her a door. A small internal page where she logs in, sees what she's allowed to see, and writes back into it. What she writes passes a check before it becomes part of the record, so she can contribute without being able to overwrite anything. This is half a day of work once you know how to ask for it, and by then you will.
3
Her own build. A separate install, her own brief, her own memory. Real, and a later conversation. Two installs diverge fast, day one they have different briefs and by day ten different versions of the truth, so a shared layer has to sit between them. That layer is separate work and I'd scope it when you need it rather than guess at it now.

My advice is to run it on yourself first. You'll know within a fortnight which of the three Rosie actually needs, and it's usually the first one.
