    A US agency that sent back slop, then a team who are better but still not at the level, don't track what happened and don't know what a test is. You said you're keen on an AI-run marketing system. You're also, reasonably, not in the mood to be sold one.

What you'd actually have

A Chief of Staff. Not an app you log into, not a service holding your data. It's Claude Code working inside one folder on your MacBook Air, and that folder is your business written down: how Pet Let runs, what you charge, who does what in which town, your voice, your standards, your decisions.

You talk to it and it does the work. Because it knows the business rather than being briefed cold every time, a decision you make once gets implemented everywhere it touches instead of becoming four conversations you now have to have.

It's yours permanently. Plain text files you can open and read. When a better model turns up next quarter you point it at the same folder and carry on, so you're never holding somebody else's platform.

Built for Pet Let, not from a template

Seventy properties with their own cleaners, maintenance, gardens, gutters and door handles, across two states. Three teams plus the Philippines back end. Forty percent direct, the rest through the platforms. That's what goes in, and it's why your install won't look like the last one I did.

Your questions, answered straight

I can't get Claude to talk to iCal. And if my team put something in the manager mailbox, it doesn't see it.

I gave you a soft answer on the call, that there is always a workaround. You deserve better than that twice, so here is a commitment instead: before the connection session I will have tested your exact setup, and I'll tell you plainly what connects properly, what needs a workaround on your own machine, and anything that genuinely doesn't work.

What I can say already: Pet Let runs on Google Workspace, and Gmail, Google Calendar and Drive are the well-trodden connections, including across more than one account. The multiple-mailbox problem is a solved one. iCal is the one to prove, and it's more promising than most because the system runs locally on your Mac alongside the app itself rather than reaching in from outside. If two-way turns out not to be clean, you'll hear it from me rather than discover it in month two.

No bullshit marketing spin. What time saving did it make for you?

You asked this exactly as my connection dropped and I had to run, so it's the first thing I owe you. A percentage would be a made-up number. And the real change wasn't time saved on things I was already doing. It was that the things I used to drop stopped getting dropped.

One from last week. My tracker said three clients were waiting on me and I looked unresponsive to all three. The system went and read the actual email threads instead of trusting my notes, and found two had already been handled and the third had approved the job weeks earlier. Three wrong records, three emails I would have sent that made me look like I hadn't been paying attention. That sort of thing, most days.

The shape to expect is a steep week, then it clicks. Your own framing is the right one and I'd hold you to that rather than a percentage: come home months further along than you planned to be by Christmas.

Is it standalone, or is it operating inside Claude?

Standalone. The system is a folder of files and instructions on your machine that a model reads and works inside. Today that model is Claude and Claude Code is the best harness for it. If that changes, the folder doesn't. You're not buying a wrapper around somebody's subscription, you're building an asset you keep.

Back it up like anything else that matters. Mine sits in a synced folder with Time Machine behind it, so a dead laptop is an inconvenience rather than an event.

Can it run the marketing, properly this time?

Yes, and here's the boundary so you can hold me to it. It does the parts your last two teams didn't: campaign structure, real variants rather than one ad reworded, the graphics and the scheduling, and tracking what actually converted so the next decision gets made on evidence. I ran my own campaigns through mine, which is how you found me.

What it won't do is decide your positioning for you, and it isn't an agency you hand the problem to. It's you running marketing properly with the labour taken off you. Given what you've paid for the other version twice, I'd rather undersell this than let you find the edge of it yourself.

This is the gateway to my entire business. Who else can see it?

Nobody. The files sit on your machine and it only sees what you point it at. Nothing comes to me and I don't hold a copy.

Two things worth saying plainly, because you'd find them later otherwise. It can see what you can see, which is the real thing to design around rather than where it's hosted, so anything that sends, spends, publishes or deletes stops and asks you first. We set those rules together, to your comfort. And on the commercial plans your content isn't used to train models, though processing does not happen in Australia today. With guest data on your books that's worth a word with whoever handles your privacy obligations. I'd rather raise it than have you find it.

What about my AI engineer, and Nick?

Your engineer is building the operations system and yours sits above it rather than across it. The clean version is that yours reads what his produces, so his work becomes the numbers on your screen and neither of you duplicates the other. He'll pick this up quickly, and honestly I'm looking forward to that conversation.

Nick is a separate install at the standing price, when you're ready and not before. His job is different from yours, so his should be built for him rather than cloned from yours. Joining the two onto one company source of truth, with the rules about who can change what, is real engineering and I'll scope and price it properly when we get there rather than guess at it now. My advice is do yours, use it, and let the shape of the rest become obvious. It will.
