Straight answers.

I've tried something like this and it didn't stick. Why would this?

Because what you tried almost certainly had no memory. Chat forgets, so every session starts from zero and you end up re-explaining your business forever, which is exhausting and eventually you stop. The architecture is the fix: your business written down once, in files on your machine, that your assistant reads before it does anything. Corrections become permanent rules instead of things you say again next week.

There's a second reason, and it's about how the first session runs. If you've been disappointed before, a generic demonstration confirms your scepticism rather than breaking it. So we don't do one. We take the job that's costing you hours right now and fix that, on your files, while you watch.

I've already built things. Is any of it wasted?

None of it. That's close to the whole point. Once the architecture is in, whatever you've already made folds into it and keeps working, whatever you built it in. What changes is that your builds start sharing one memory, so the next one takes a fraction of the time the last one did. People arrive braced to be told to start again and it's the opposite.

Could I just build this myself?

Yes, and some people should. If you enjoy this and you've got the evenings, sit down with Claude Code and you'll get there in months and months. That's not me being modest, it's what it actually took me.

What you're buying is the year of wrong turns already taken. Where memory lives, what gets read first, what your assistant must never do without asking. Those decisions don't look important until one of them is wrong, and by then you've built on top of it. If your time is worth more than the months, take one of the three. If it isn't, take the kit and do it yourself with the map in your hand.

Do I need to be technical?

No, and I'd be the wrong person to teach you if you did. I've never written code. I ran video production companies and before that I was a scientist. Everything here is written in plain English by someone who had to learn it as a non-programmer, which is exactly why it can be taught to one.

Why is the room cheaper than the one to one?

Because eleven other people are paying for the same two days. That's the only reason. You get the same architecture and the same arc; what you don't get is four weeks of my full attention on your business alone, which is what the guided build is and what it costs.

Won't this be out of date in six months? AI moves fast.

The models will change, and that's fine, because the models are the part you rent. What you own is the architecture: your business written down, your rules, your memory, your files. Point it at a better model next year and it gets better. That's the design, not a hope.

On the build and on the two days, you get every update for twelve months. The kit gets the version current at purchase, and if a later one is offered I'll say so, but it isn't promised.

Where does my data live, and am I locked in?

On your machine, in plain files you can open, read and change. Not on a platform of mine, not in an account you'd lose access to. You can back it up, move it to another computer, point it at a different AI company, or delete the lot. There's no ongoing fee to me and nothing to cancel.

A Mac or a Windows PC, a paid Claude plan in your own name, and two small free background tools, Git and Python. You'll never open either one.
