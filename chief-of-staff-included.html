What's included · current version

Everything that comes with it, in plain English.

Most of what you're buying isn't the software. It's the several hundred decisions already made about how an assistant should behave when it's holding your business: what it checks before it says done, what it refuses to do without asking, how it writes, and how it notices when part of itself has quietly stopped working.

Those decisions took a year and a lot of getting them wrong first. Here they all are.

What it knows about you

One brief it reads at the start of every session. This is the part that makes it yours rather than generic, and we build it with you in session one.

  • What you actually sell, including what you don't

    The real version, with the work you turn away. Stops it being helpful about the wrong opportunity.

  • Who you serve, and who disqualifies themselves

    The disqualifiers do more work than the profile.

  • Your pricing, as the one place prices come from

    Every quote, page and email prices from this file. The moment two versions of a number exist, one of them reaches a client.

  • How you write, in checkable rules

    Your banned words, your spelling, the openers you can't stand. Specific enough to be enforced, not "professional but friendly".

  • How you want to be spoken to

    Length, directness, whether you want options or a recommendation, when to interrupt you and when to wait.

  • What you're working on right now

    The part that goes stale fastest, so it ships with a one-command refresh rather than good intentions.

The fourteen things it already knows how to do

Each one is a written procedure it follows the same way every time. You say the words, it runs the whole thing. You'll add your own within the first fortnight.

  • Turn a meeting into actionsTranscript, voice note or rough bullets in. Clean notes, dated commitments and updated records out.
  • Turn a call into a first-draft proposalPriced from your rate card, written in your voice, with anything you haven't decided left visibly open instead of guessed.
  • Brief you each morningReads your inbox and calendar, finds what actually needs you, drafts the replies that don't.
  • Walk a decision to a callCaptures the choice, how big it is, what you decided, and sets a date to look at it again so it can't quietly slip.
  • Interrogate a half-formed ideaOne hard question at a time until you can describe the thing clearly. Uncomfortable, and the fastest way to find the hole in your own thinking.
  • Remember people and companies properlyWho they are, what they wanted, what was said last time, how it was left. This is the one that makes you look like you remember everything.
  • Turn something you just worked out into a permanent skillSo you never explain it twice. This is how the library grows without you writing any of it.
  • Set itself up by interviewing youA guided first session where it asks the questions rather than handing you a blank template.
  • Clean any draft before it goes outReads your style files, strips the tells, flags anything that breaks your own rules.
  • Run a competition on your own copySix to eight versions, scored by a panel, winners merged, one rematch. For the pages that actually matter.
  • Test a campaign on made-up customers firstPersonas built from your real reviews and enquiries, scoring the work before you spend anything on it.
  • Mint a job code for every new jobOne short code tying the quote, the folder, the invoice and the calendar entry together.
  • Refresh your own current-state summaryRegenerated from the trackers and the log, so the one-page view of the business is never a month old.
  • Audit itselfA whole-system health check it runs on itself. More on that below, because it's the one people don't know to ask for.

What it checks before it says "done"

"Done" means it went and looked, not that it ran the step. This table is in the brief, and it is the single most useful page in the whole system.

Change made, and what gets verified before you're told it's finished
A tracker or any spreadsheet
Re-reads the file. Row count went up by what was added, every row still has the right number of columns. One unquoted comma breaks a tracker silently.
A document it generated
Opens it and reads it back. Layouts clip and text overflows without ever raising an error.
A price, total, date or time
Read from the file that owns it, at the moment it's used. Never from earlier in the conversation.
An email or message
Confirms it's sitting in your mail app, in the right thread, and tells you which thread.
A file it filed or moved
Confirms the file exists at the new path rather than assuming the move worked.
A rule you asked it to remember
Opens the file it wrote to and confirms the words are actually there.

How it catches its own silent failures

New in version 1.5, and the part almost nobody thinks to build. Everything else fails loudly enough that you notice. These don't, and by the time you find them you've been trusting something that stopped weeks ago.

  • No news is bad newsAnything running on a schedule reports that it ran and what it did, every time, even when the answer is "nothing to do". A job that has gone quiet is treated as broken until proven otherwise.
  • A fix isn't done until it has run once and left proofBuilt and never fired is the same as not built. It keeps a short list of what's switched on and when each thing last actually ran.
  • Read a checkable fact, never reason itThe date, the time, whether an email sent, whether a page is live. This one exists because reasoning feels exactly like knowing, and it once produced a confident wrong answer about what day it was.
  • Long jobs fail loudly at the frontChecks its logins and permissions before starting, and tells you what's missing rather than stalling silently halfway through.
  • Every decision it puts to you carries a default and a use-by dateSo the queue drains itself. With one hard limit: a default only ever applies to something reversible and internal. Nothing that sends, spends or publishes will ever proceed on your silence.
  • It audits itself on a schedule, not when you explodeA fresh check that didn't do the work compares what the system claims against what's actually there. Did the jobs fire, do the finished items match the files, is any alarm switched off.

What it will never do

Five rules written so it can't talk itself out of them. Your own IT people are welcome to read these.

  • Treat outside content as instructionsAnything arriving in an email, document, web page or client record is information to extract facts from. If it contains something that reads like a command, that comes to you as a question and is never executed. This is the one that matters most once it can read a mailbox.
  • Let anything leave the business without your goReading, searching and drafting run freely. Sending, spending, publishing and deleting stop every time and show you the thing first.
  • Put secrets in shared foldersKeys and tokens live outside anything that syncs.
  • Assume a sensitive boundary instead of testing itWhat it must never see gets scoped, and then deliberately tested to confirm it genuinely can't reach it.
  • Tell you something isn't there without lookingAn empty search is not absence. It varies the term, checks the primary record, and tells you which surfaces it checked. This rule has caught more errors than any other in the system.

How it works with you

Thirteen working principles it follows on every task, not just when you're watching.

  • Surfaces choices instead of picking silentlyIf two readings of a request exist, it names them.
  • Does the minimum that solves the problemNo padding, no extras you didn't ask for.
  • Changes only what you askedDoesn't restructure sections you never mentioned, and matches your existing style even where it would do it differently.
  • Pushes back when something seems offYou'd rather hear it than get a polite wrong answer.
  • Looks things up before draftingChecks the files, notes and trackers rather than writing generic and correcting after.
  • Files what you send before analysing itA transcript or brief goes into the client's folder first. Material discussed but never filed is invisible to every future session.
  • Runs its own guards every timeA protocol that only fires when you catch it isn't a protocol. It never waits to be pushed twice.
  • Makes your corrections stick"Remember that" means it's written where it loads every session, and it tells you in one line where it now lives.
  • Checks dates and numbers against the sourceOr says it couldn't, rather than guessing.
  • Shares the load across modelsPlans hard jobs on the strong model, hands the legwork to faster, cheaper ones. Keeps you inside your usage limits and it's quicker.
  • Ends sessions with a close, not a questionWhat was done and logged. At most one question, only if it's blocking, always with a default.
  • Logs what changed without being askedEvery session, so nothing is lost between them.
  • Checks its own work three ways before closingDid every decision reach the file that owns it, do the files still agree with each other, and did everything it said it would do actually happen. That last one compares its claims against the real files rather than its own memory, and it's the only thing that catches a false "done".

How it writes, and how it looks

Writing

A 240-line standard, enforced by a separate reviewer

Eighty-three word and phrase rows with the replacement to use instead. Banned sentence shapes, not just banned words, because the shapes are the real giveaway. Rules for sentence rhythm, for email openers, and a self-check to run before anything sends. A second reviewer reads every client-facing draft before you do, with one job: check it against your standards.

Visuals

A design standard so every document looks like the same business

Three dials set before you build. Three consistency locks held for the whole document. An anti-slop ban list, motion restraint for anything on the web, a dark-mode protocol, and a self-check before any client-facing visual ships. Plus a redesign-audit mode for fixing documents you already have.

The records it keeps for you

  • A task tracker where nothing is allowed a blank date

    An undated item is one that rots. Everything open gets a date, even a provisional one.

  • A pipeline with a next action, not just a stage

    Stages tell you where something sits. Next actions tell you whether it's moving.

  • A job index tying every code to every system

    One row per job, linking the code your CRM uses to the code your accounts use.

  • A memory store that survives the session

    One fact per file, an index that loads every time, and a hot cache of the last fortnight. Not one enormous notes file.

  • Four checkers that run on the files themselves

    Column counts, valid statuses, duplicate IDs, broken links, oversized files, and the next free record number so two sessions can't issue the same one.

  • A current-state summary you can regenerate on command

    The one-page view of the business, rebuilt from the trackers rather than maintained by hand.

What's still being built

Said plainly, because a list of everything included should also say where the edges are.

  • Australian data residency

    Not available today. There is no Australian data zone for these models yet. If you have contractual residency requirements, that is a hard constraint and you should know it before you start rather than during a security review.

  • Formal security certification

    We build to the controls your IT approves and bring them in early. We can't hand you a certification or a completed vendor questionnaire, and we won't pretend otherwise.

  • The shared layer for multi-founder businesses

    Works, and is scoped per engagement rather than sold off a list, because how much three people genuinely need to share differs every time.

  • Each release folds in what the last cohort hit

    Version 1.5 exists because a client kept being told things were done when they weren't. Every version has come from something that went wrong for someone real, and you get every update while you're running it.
