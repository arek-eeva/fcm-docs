WHY NOT JUST CHATGPT?

You're already good at ChatGPT. So was I.

Work at it and it writes a genuinely decent email. That was never the problem.

The problem is that everything a chat window makes lands back on your desk. Prompt, wait, read, copy, paste, fix, send. It remembers things about you, but it doesn't hold your business: every client, every price, every promise. In a chat window, every output is a dead end you carry forward alone. In a system, it isn't.

You feel it when something changes. You update your prices, and now the pricing sheet is right and eleven proposals are wrong, and you're the one fixing them. A Chief of Staff carries that one decision through every document that depends on it. Change it once, everything downstream follows.

You also only say things once. I told it I don't use em dashes, and that was the end of it. And it takes correction without ego. When it does get something wrong it tells you what it missed and notes to check both places next time. No defensiveness, no apologising three times, no working around its feelings.

That cost never arrives as a bill. It arrives in instalments, as evenings, and as starting over. One founder told me he'd been at this three years. Not ignoring it, trying: "We've started and stopped a few times." He wasn't behind. He'd been paying the whole time.

Straight answers.

I've tried this before and it didn't stick. Why is this different?

Because most people are told to start small, and small is where it dies. You build one tidy thing, it works, and it sits on its own with nothing to connect to, so the next thing starts from scratch again. That's not a discipline problem, it's an architecture problem. We start with the architecture, so the first thing you build has somewhere to live and the second one is faster because of the first. If you've already built things, they get folded in rather than replaced.

What's the catch?

The honest one is what happens two or three weeks in, when it clicks. People stop sleeping properly. You'll find yourself running six things at once because the cost of not building the idea you just had feels too high, and you'll cancel things you shouldn't. It passes, and what's left is a system built exactly the way you want it, but nobody warns you about that stretch and it's worth knowing it's normal.

Won't this be out of date in six months? AI moves fast.

The opposite. It isn't an app that ships once and dates. It's a set of instructions that runs on whatever the best model is that week, so when a sharper model lands, your system gets sharper the same day. No rebuild, no new invoice.

Where does my data live, and what can the system see?

The files live on your own machine, not on my servers or anyone's platform. When it works on something, the model reads what it needs in that moment, the same way text you paste into ChatGPT does, and you set the rules about what it can and can't touch. The harder version of this, what it might write down that you'd rather it forgot, is the next answer.

What about the things it shouldn't remember?

That worry has a precise shape, and it isn't hacking. It's the system quietly writing something down that a person would have had the discretion to forget. A client who mentions a death in the family. A staff member venting about a colleague.

Discretion here is a written rule you set, in plain language, during the build: what it may hold, what it must never write down, and what always needs your eyes before it goes anywhere. The rules live in a file you can open and read, and we decide up front where anything it writes lands, so a summary can't end up somewhere looser than the conversation it came from.

Two honest limits. A rule is only as good as the wording you give it, so we write yours together during the build and tighten it as we go. And nobody, me included, has solved every case of a system inferring something across records you've already given it. If your work carries obligations a written rule can't satisfy, listed-company clients or insurer conditions, this build isn't the right tool for that part of it, and I'll tell you that on the call rather than after the invoice.

What does it cost to run after the build?

The model subscription, paid to the AI provider at their standard rate. No retainer to me, no per-seat licence, no platform fee. If you never want to see me again after the fortnight, the system doesn't care.

Will it keep working while I'm away from the computer?

Some jobs run automatically, provided the machine running them stays on and awake. Other jobs run as a session you start and leave. We set up the right mode for each job during the build, so you know what runs on its own and what you start.

Do I need to be technical?

You won't be writing code. You should be comfortable with files, browser tabs, installing an application and following a setup checklist. If you already use ChatGPT or Claude most days and can explain how your business works, you're in the right range.

Am I locked in to you, or to one AI company?

Neither, by design. Everything sits in plain files you own, it can move to another model or harness with the system itself walking you through it, and there's no retainer holding you anywhere. My whole job is to make myself unnecessary.
