Another way to think about the price

You're hiring, not subscribing.

Set the software framing aside for a second. What you're putting in place is something you can hand most of your computer work to. The drafting, the chasing, the filing, the looking-up, the remembering who said what in March. It works to your rules, in your voice, on your own records, and it's there at eleven o'clock on a Sunday night when you finally get to your inbox.

It costs $5,500 plus GST once, and then about a hundred dollars a month to Anthropic to keep it running. That's the whole cost. No seat licence to me, no per-user pricing, no annual renewal that creeps.

Hold that against what the same work costs you now. Either you're doing it yourself in the hours you'd rather spend somewhere else, or you're paying someone to do it, with the recruiting, the training, the managing and the handover when they leave. I'm not going to put a salary figure on this page and pretend it's your number, because it isn't. Do the sum against your own.

Where the comparison stops

It doesn't have judgement and I won't pretend it does. It won't sit in a room with a client, make a call you'd want a person to make, or take responsibility for anything. What it takes off you is the two or three hours a day that never needed your judgement in the first place. That's the honest version, and it's still the best-value hire you'll make this year.

The part worth saying out loud

What a day gets you, and what it doesn't.

A day gets you the foundation and the method, properly, and you leave with a system running on your own machine. It doesn't get you everything four sessions get you. The depth comes over the weeks after, from you using it. Anyone who tells you a single day replaces months of work is selling you something.

The room and the one to one cost the same, and I'll give you the real reason rather than the marketing one. They cost me about the same to deliver, and if I priced the room lower it would become the budget option, which would quietly punish everyone who can't travel to Melbourne. So the choice between them is your calendar, not your budget.

The question you're actually asking

Could you just build this yourself?

Yes, and I'd rather say so on my own sales page than have you work it out later. All of it is a folder, some plain files, and rules written in English. There's no product you have to buy to make it work, and I held nothing back today.

What it costs you is months of evenings, and about a hundred decisions where the wrong answer stays invisible for six months. Where your memory lives. What your assistant reads first. What it never does without asking you. What it does when two records disagree about the same client. Each one has a right answer and about four plausible wrong ones, and the wrong ones don't announce themselves. They just quietly cost you the compounding.

Plenty of people in that room should do exactly that, and if you're one of them, good. Everything on this page is for the people who'd rather not spend the year finding out which four were wrong.

Straight answers.

Why is the room the same price as the one to one?

Because they cost me about the same to run, and because pricing the room lower would make it the budget option. That would turn a day in a room into a luxury tax on everyone who can't travel to Melbourne, which is backwards. Pick on your calendar: a whole day with nine other founders, or about five hours spread over four weeks on your own business from wherever you are.

How much of your time am I actually getting?

On the build, about five hours across the four sessions, plus the two follow-ups after handover and stuck-support in between when you're jammed. On the day, a full working day plus a fortnight plan and two weeks of support after it. I'd rather you did that division yourself than take a vague answer, because what you're really paying for isn't the hours, it's which hundred decisions get made correctly inside them.

Can I start with the kit and upgrade later?

Yes, and it costs you nothing to do it that way if you move within 30 days, because what you paid for the kit comes off the price of a build or a seat in full. Nothing you set up in the meantime gets thrown away either, since the kit is the same architecture the build starts from. After 30 days it's a separate purchase.

What if I'm not in Melbourne?

Take the guided build. Same system, four sessions and two follow-ups over about four weeks, remote, and it's more custom than the day because yours is the only business in the room. If you'd rather fly in for the day, that's a real option, and the drinks after are a fair part of why.

What do I actually keep at the end?

Your own files, on your own machine, and your business written in plain English you can read and change. Everything you build with it is yours outright. The kit itself you hold under a perpetual licence rather than owning its copyright, which is set out in the terms.

There's no platform to log into and no licence fee to me. The one honest dependency is that it runs on a paid Claude plan, so you're relying on Anthropic the way you already rely on your accounting software. If you never speak to me again it keeps working, and I built it that way on purpose.

Do I get updates after we finish?

On the Melbourne day and the guided build, yes. You get every update to the system for twelve months from when we start, as they're released, and there's nothing extra to pay for them. I'm improving this constantly because I run my own businesses on it, so what you'll get over that year is the same work I'm doing for myself.

The kit is different, and I'd rather be straight about it than let you find out later. A kit buyer gets the version current at the date of purchase. If a later version is offered to existing kit licensees I'll say so plainly at the time, but it isn't automatic and it isn't promised.

What's the catch?

There is one, and I'd rather say it plainly. Two or three weeks in, when it clicks, people stop sleeping properly. You'll find yourself running six things at once because the cost of not building the idea you just had feels too high, and you'll cancel things you shouldn't. It wears off, and what's left is a system built exactly the way you want it. Few people selling this mention that stretch. I just did.

What do I need before any of this works?

A Mac or a Windows PC, a paid Claude plan in your own name, and Git installed. Git is a small free background tool you'll never open, and installing it takes a few minutes. It's the one part of setup nobody can do for you remotely, because it's a dialog someone has to click at your own keyboard. On the build and the day, we do it together.
