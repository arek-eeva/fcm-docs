Straight answers.

What is this actually worth once it's running?

I can only tell you what it's done in my own business, because that's the one I have a year of records for. My system keeps a ledger of every job it does and what that job would have cost me otherwise. In July alone it did 72.5 hours of work I'd have done myself, and $159,762 of work I'd have paid someone else to do. That's one month.

The honest caveat, because you should have it: 83% of that dollar figure is priced at rates I actually pay or charge, and the other 17% is my estimate of what I'd have been quoted. There's a third category the ledger keeps separate and doesn't count, another $18,864 of work I'd have wanted and never justified buying. That's the part that surprised me most, and it isn't money saved, it's things that simply become possible.

Your numbers will be your own. What I'd say is that the kit at $950 is roughly two hours of most people's charge-out rate.

I've already built things. Does any of it carry over?

All of it, and this is the part people don't expect. Once the system is installed, anything you've already built folds into it, whatever you built it in. The system reads it, uses it, and works around it. You lose nothing.

If anything you come out ahead, because the pieces that were sitting on their own start talking to each other. That's usually the moment it clicks: the proposal generator and the pricing sheet and the thing that chews through call transcripts stop being three separate tools you have to remember to open, and start being one system that already knows what the others did.

Two days gets you the foundation and the method properly, and you leave with a system running on your own machine and the confidence to keep building it. What it doesn't get you is everything four one-to-one sessions do, because those are spent entirely on your business. That depth comes over the weeks after, from using it. Could I just build this myself?

Yes, and I'd rather say so on my own sales page. It's a folder, plain files, and rules in English. Nothing was held back today.

It costs months of evenings and about a hundred decisions where the wrong answer stays invisible for six months. Each has one right answer and four plausible wrong ones, and the wrong ones don't announce themselves. They quietly cost you the compounding. Plenty of people should do it anyway, and if that's you, good.

Because eleven other founders are paying for the same day of my time. That's the whole reason, and it's why the room is the better buy for most people. What do I keep at the end?

Your files, on your machine, and your business in plain English you can change. What you build is yours outright. The kit itself you hold under a perpetual licence rather than owning its copyright. What's the catch?

Two or three weeks in, when it clicks, people stop sleeping properly. You'll run six things at once and cancel things you shouldn't. It wears off. Few people selling this mention that stretch. I just did.

What do I need before it works?

A Mac or a Windows PC, a paid Claude plan in your own name, and two small free background tools, Git and Python. You'll never open either one. A few minutes to install on a Mac, where Python comes along with Git, and a couple more on Windows, where it's a separate download. The kit's instructions walk you through both.
